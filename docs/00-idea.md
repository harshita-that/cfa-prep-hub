# 00 — The Idea

> **What is this doc?** The very first step of any project: write the idea down in plain English *before* building anything. If you can't explain it simply, you're not ready to build it.

## One-sentence pitch

An affordable, focused practice platform that helps CFA candidates study smarter — short daily practice, clear progress, no fluff.

## The problem

- CFA prep is **expensive**. Popular prep providers charge hundreds of dollars per level.
- Candidates are often **working full-time** and study in small pockets (commute, lunch break, late night).
- The curriculum is **huge** (10 topic areas, thousands of pages), and it's hard to know *where you stand* and *what to study next*.
- Many tools are bloated: long videos, cluttered dashboards, too many features.

## Target users

| User | Description |
|---|---|
| **Primary:** Working-professional candidate | 22–35, studying for Level 1 while working. Studies 30–60 min on weekdays, more on weekends. Mostly on phone. |
| **Secondary:** Student candidate | Final-year university student taking Level 1. Price-sensitive. |
| **Later:** Level 2 / Level 3 candidates | Same needs, harder content. |

## What makes it different

1. **Affordable** — far cheaper than big prep providers (or free tier + low-cost premium).
2. **Focused** — practice-first. Flashcards, questions, mock exams. Not hours of video.
3. **Mobile-first** — designed for 10-minute study sessions on a phone.
4. **Honest progress** — shows weak topics, weighted by how much they count on the exam.
5. **(Later) Smart scheduling** — spaced repetition shows you cards right before you'd forget them.

## What success looks like

- **MVP (this repo):** a working site we're proud to show, and both founders can confidently use Git, GitHub, branches, and PRs.
- **Real product, first 6 months:** 100 real candidates using it weekly, and at least 10 saying "this helped me pass".
- **Long-term:** a sustainable business that candidates recommend to each other.

## Open questions for Aayush and his friend

> These are real decisions only the founders can make. Answer them in a Pull Request (see Issue: *Answer the Open questions*). It's fine to write "not sure yet — here's what we're leaning towards".

1. **Which levels first?** Level 1 only, or Level 1–3 from the start? (Level 1 has the most candidates; Levels 2–3 are harder to write content for.)
2. **Pricing model?** Free with ads, freemium (free basics + paid mock exams), one-time payment per level, or monthly subscription? What price feels fair to a student in India vs. a professional in the US?
3. **Where do the questions come from?** Who writes them? CFA Institute material is copyrighted — we **cannot** copy their questions. Do we write originals, hire charterholders, or license content?
4. **Web app or mobile app?** A website that works great on phones, a native app (App Store / Play Store), or both? (Web first is much cheaper and faster.)
5. **Who are the first 20 users?** Do you know CFA candidates personally? Study groups, LinkedIn, Reddit (r/CFA), college clubs?
6. **What's the one feature that would make someone switch** from what they use today?
7. **Who does what?** Who owns content, who owns product/design, who owns code, who talks to users?
8. **How much time can each of you commit per week**, and what's the target date for a first real version?
